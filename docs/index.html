What's a neighborhood tabulation area? New York City has no official neighborhood borders, so the Department of City Planning draws neighborhood tabulation areas (NTAs) — 262 statistical areas built from whole 2020 census tracts, sized so census survey data stays reliable. This board ranks the 197 residential ones and leaves out parks, cemeteries, airports and Rikers Island. The names are City Planning's, and an NTA doesn't always match what locals call the place — some neighborhoods are split (Bedford-Stuyvesant East and West), others merged. Tap any neighborhood's row to see its boundary on a map, or read the methodology.
