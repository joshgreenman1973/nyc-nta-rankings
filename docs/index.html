What's a neighborhood tabulation area? New York City has no official neighborhood borders, so the Department of City Planning draws neighborhood tabulation areas (NTAs) — 262 statistical areas built from whole 2020 census tracts, sized so census survey data stays reliable. This board ranks the 197 residential ones and leaves out parks, cemeteries, airports and Rikers Island. NTAs are counting units, not neighborhoods in the everyday sense. Where one neighborhood ends and the next begins is hotly disputed, as The New York Times found when it asked tens of thousands of New Yorkers to draw their own. City Planning's lines and names often won't match what residents call the place: some neighborhoods are split (Bedford-Stuyvesant East and West), others are merged, and a block you'd call one neighborhood may be counted in another.
